Continuation of the ChatGPT thread "UK AI Remote Jobs Research". It started as a
search for UK work-from-home jobs where a bespoke AI agent could do most of the
work. The thread settled on *ordinary* admin, data and HR jobs rather than
AI-labelled ones.

Research date: 25 October 2026. Where I could, I opened the employer's own
vacancy page. Automation scores are my estimates, not measurements.

## What I found by searching

- **Genuinely remote, part-time, data-processing jobs are scarce.** My searches
  of Indeed, Reed, Glassdoor, CV-Library and Jobsite found few listings. Most
  results were full-time, hybrid or office-based, or were old, reposted or
  duplicated adverts.
  - Asda Master Data Administrator (Leeds): part-time, 25 h/week, closes 27 Oct, but it's a store-based retail post.
  - Data Protection Admin Assistant: fully remote, but 40 h/week at £30-35k.
  - Accounts payable: Robert Half's part-time purchase-ledger post was 2 years old. The Hays temp role is full-time.
  - Upwork has freelance invoice-processing gigs, some under 30 h/week.
- **Job-board counts are inflated.** Headline numbers such as "13,647 remote data-entry jobs" include duplicates, aggregator reposts and non-jobs.
- **Better route: freelance or contract work.** For example, bulk document extraction, invoice-to-spreadsheet conversion, CRM clean-ups and catalogue data. The client pays for the finished result, so building automation to produce it is the point of the work.

6. Content or Merchandising Auditor (like VoucherCodes)
7. HR Administrator (mostly for onboarding paperwork and records; the better-paid roles want experience)

## The employer-rules problem

The Reddit threads about people automating their jobs are anecdotes, not
evidence, and many of those people only automate part of the work. UK employers
increasingly have AI-use policies (approved tools, banned data types, mandatory
human review). Contracts also commonly include confidentiality, data-protection
and IT-security clauses. UK GDPR limits significant automated decisions about
people, so HR and payroll work in particular would need human sign-off.

I couldn't find UK sources on whether an employee must disclose that they've
automated their own role. That depends on the contract. Before building
anything:

1. Read the contract and the AI/IT policy.
2. Ask the employer if automation is allowed. Some will welcome it, especially for backlog or data-migration work.
3. Don't feed customer, HR or financial data into third-party AI tools without permission.

That's also why contract and freelance work is the safer first target.

## Other things we could do

1. **Freelance extraction and reconciliation gigs.** [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/data-extraction-freelancers/eg/)
   has live listings for invoice posting, bank reconciliation and PDF-to-spreadsheet
   conversion. I found no live UK PeoplePerHour or Freelancer.co.uk equivalents
   by search, so check those boards directly. Here the client pays for the result,
   so building an agent is the job.
2. **Contract ecommerce and product-data work.** One charity ecommerce admin
   contract (fully remote, 3 days a week, £150-160/day, Shopify uploads and feeds)
   showed up, but I couldn't confirm it's still open. Most other product-data and
   data-cleansing listings were old, hybrid or full-time.
3. **A small automation service for UK small businesses.** The demand drivers are
   real (Making Tax Digital, inbox and invoice admin), but I only found vendor
   marketing, with no reliable pricing or demand figures. The UK GDPR duties of
   processing clients' emails and invoices would need checking first.
4. **Build a portfolio agent first.** A document-to-spreadsheet pipeline (OCR,
   field extraction, validation, exception log) on sample invoices shows what you
   can do to employers and clients, and lets you test reliability before relying
   on it.
